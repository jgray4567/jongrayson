What I do

Twenty-five years designing for complex organizations, from small product teams to enterprise platforms. Every engagement starts with the same question, “What breaks if this is wrong?”

Experience design

End-to-end experience design grounded in research: usability testing, heuristic evaluation, journey mapping, and contextual inquiry, including inside classified and high-security environments. Building design organizations, establishing governance, and connecting UX outcomes to business metrics.

Product design

Product direction from first concept to shipped release. Defining what gets built and in what order, then designing the flows, states, and edge cases the roadmap depends on.

AI integration

Interfaces where the model does the work and the person stays in control. Designed for trust, transparency, and appropriate reliance rather than blind acceptance.

Design systems

Component frameworks adopted across multi-agency programs serving 2,000+ users. Consistency at enterprise scale without slowing the teams that build on them.

My UX leadership philosophy

I’ve spent my career helping organizations build products that people actually enjoy using.

Sometimes that meant redesigning complex software. Sometimes it meant building a UX team from the ground up. Sometimes it meant helping Product and Engineering stop working in parallel and start solving problems together.

Read the rest Show less

What I’ve learned is that good UX isn’t about making screens prettier. It’s about making products easier to understand, easier to trust, and easier to use. When you get that right, customers stay longer, teams move faster, and the business wins.

I enjoy building teams just as much as building products. I like mentoring designers, creating simple processes that help everyone do better work, and giving people room to grow without getting in their way.

Over the past few years, a lot of my focus has shifted toward AI. Not because it’s the latest trend, but because it’s another tool that can help people make better decisions and remove busy work. The important part isn’t the technology. It’s knowing when AI adds value and when good human judgment matters more.

I also believe the best UX organizations don’t operate on the sidelines. They have a seat at the table from the beginning. The best ideas come from Product, Engineering, Design, Research, and Content thinking through problems together, not tossing work over the wall to the next team.

If there’s one thing I’ve consistently tried to build throughout my career, it’s alignment. Better conversations. Better decisions. Better products.

The tools will change. AI will change. Customer expectations will keep changing.

The job stays the same.

Understand the people you’re building for, build a team that enjoys solving hard problems together, and never lose sight of why you’re building the product in the first place.

Intelligence Layer

A client ran a real intelligence dashboard program and asked: could I design and build something that pulls nine incompatible feeds together and makes them make sense to somebody who has never seen the screen before?

Intel Layer is the answer. Air traffic, orbital assets, seismic events, weather warnings and reference geography on one 3D Earth. Every feed is public, live, and named on screen. Nothing is mocked, because mock data lets a designer avoid every problem that matters.

How the work runs

Every engagement starts by identifying the failure that matters most, then works backwards from that answer.

  1. Intelligence gathering

    Stakeholder mental models, workflow failure points, existing tools, and what people actually do versus what they say they do.

  2. Systems architecture

    Information architecture, AI integration mapping, and component logic, absorbing complexity so the people using the system do not have to.

  3. Prototyping and validation

    High-fidelity prototypes a team can stress-test before code is written. Flaws cost hours here rather than months later.

  4. Delivery and capability

    Embedding with teams, establishing governance, and building capability that outlasts the engagement.
