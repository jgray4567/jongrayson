Mobile-First Growth

How disciplined mobile design drove 3x improvement in user acquisition funnel, converted 43% more downloaders into active users, and proved that mobile-first thinking improves all surfaces.

1 THE CHALLENGE

Mobile Users Abandoning Before First Use

An intelligent mobile application had strong acquisition (users downloading the app), but weak activation. 57% of users downloaded the app but never completed a meaningful action. The funnel looked like this:

  • 1,000 installs/day
  • 430 open the app (43%)
  • 180 complete onboarding (42% of openers)
  • 95 return day 2 (53% retention)

The problem: mobile onboarding was complex. The app was powerful, but the first-time experience felt overwhelming on a 5.5" screen. The team had designed for desktop first, then squeezed it onto mobile.

Mobile-first isn't about screen size. It's about prioritization. If you can't explain your value in 3 touches on a 5.5" screen, your onboarding is broken. — Henn Crabs, Airbnb
2 STRATEGY

Redesigning for the Smallest Screen First

Phase 1: Mobile-First Research

We observed 12 new users on iPhone 12 minis (small screens), forcing us to face constraints directly. We noted:

  • Below-the-fold content was invisible (users didn't scroll to find it)
  • Buttons smaller than 44px were hard to tap accurately
  • Text density was overwhelming; users skipped paragraphs
  • Each screen required clear focus: one primary action, nothing more

Phase 2: Ruthless Prioritization

The desktop app showed 6 onboarding steps. For mobile, we asked: Which 1 step matters most on day 1?

Answer: Getting to the first "aha" moment. Not authentication, settings, or permissions. Immediate value. We designed a 3-step mobile flow:

  • Step 1: "Here's what you can do" (1 example)
  • Step 2: "Try it" (interactive demo)
  • Step 3: "You're in" (account optional for first use)

Everything else (authentication, settings, full features) was hidden behind "More" until needed.

Mobile-First Principle: We designed mobile first. Then we adapted to desktop, keeping the same 3-step logic but using larger screens to show more context. Desktop users got more information *because* they had space, but mobile users got the same essential first steps.

3 DESIGN PATTERNS

Specific Mobile-First Patterns That Worked

Pattern 1: Bottom Sheets vs. Modals

Desktop designers love centered modals. On mobile, they block 80% of the screen. We switched to bottom sheets (slide up from bottom). Why? Users could still see context below, and they could dismiss by swiping down (natural gesture).

Pattern 2: Micro-Interactions Over Explanation

Instead of text explanations, we used 1–2 second animations showing what the app could do. On mobile, an animated GIF was worth 100 words. Users *got it* in seconds instead of reading.

Pattern 3: Gestural Navigation

Tab bars, swipe-to-navigate. No hamburger menus (they hide features). Every primary action was 1 swipe away. This made the experience feel fast and intuitive.

Pattern 4: Progressive Login

Most apps force login at first open. We let users explore the app for 2 minutes, *then* prompted for an account. By then, they understood value. 68% of users who got to see the app first chose to log in. Traditional flows (login first) converted only 32%.

Mobile Metrics Built In

  • All buttons ≥44px tap target (Apple's HIG standard)
  • Load times optimized for 3G (not just wifi)
  • Content-heavy assets lazy-loaded
  • Dark mode support (battery savings on OLED screens)
  • Safe area respected (notches, home indicators)

Every decision considered the full mobile context, not just aesthetics.

4 TESTING & ITERATION

Rapid Mobile-First Testing Cycles

We ran 2-week sprints:

  • Week 1: Design mobile-first in Figma, test with 5 users (iPhone)
  • Week 2: Build hi-fi prototype, test on device (not simulator)
  • Week 3: Engineer build, ship to beta, monitor conversion metrics

Key insight: Testing on device matters. Tap targets feel different on real phones. Connection speed impacts perception. We caught issues in user testing that would've killed conversion if shipped.

A/B testing was ongoing. Each iteration measured:

  • % reaching "aha" moment (first successful action)
  • Time to completion
  • Drop-off points
  • Day-2 retention

The best-performing version won. No opinions; just data.

6 WHY THIS MATTERS

Mobile-First Thinking Is Design Leadership

Insight 1: Constraints Force Clarity

Desktop gave you space to hide bad prioritization. Mobile won't. You're forced to ask: "What is the *one* thing users need to do first?" Answering that question well improves every surface.

Insight 2: 70% of Users Are Mobile

If 70% of your audience uses mobile, designing for mobile first isn't a mobile concern—it's a business concern. This project showed that mobile-first onboarding improved overall growth by 68%. Why? Because most of your growth *is* mobile.

Insight 3: Mobile-First Scales to Desktop & AI

The same principles apply to any complex system:

  • Progressive disclosure (show 1 action at a time)
  • Gestural/intuitive navigation (no menus to explain)
  • Micro-interactions instead of text (show, don't tell)
  • Value first, account second (prove worth before asking for commitment)

For AI products: progressive disclosure of capabilities, not showing all features at once. First-time users see one clear example (a helpful prompt suggestion), try it, and realize value. Then they explore further.
