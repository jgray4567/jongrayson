Designing for Trust in AI

Three years of building interfaces where artificial intelligence amplifies human capability without replacing judgment. The same principles apply whether you're designing intelligence analysis tools or consumer assistants.

Core Principles for AI UX

  • Transparency Over Magic: Users need to understand why AI suggested something. Plain-language reasoning, not black-box suggestions.
  • Confidence Scoring: Show how certain the AI is. Let users calibrate their trust based on data.
  • User Control: Override, undo, and correct. Reframe AI as assistant, not decision-maker.
  • Progressive Disclosure: Don't show all AI capabilities at once. Teach by doing, reveal as competence grows.
  • Human-in-the-Loop: Let users teach the AI through corrections. AI improves, users feel agency.
  • Mobile-First Reasoning: Complex AI reasoning must be digestible on small screens. If you can explain it in 2 lines, it's clear.

These principles drove adoption in intelligence analysis platforms, mission-critical workflows, and research applications. They apply to any AI product's first-mile challenge.

Intellectual Foundations

The researchers, designers, and thinkers whose work shaped these approaches. Standing on the shoulders of people who did the foundational thinking.

Key Influences & Frameworks

Don Norman
(Design of Everyday Things)
Affordances, constraints, and feedback. If something feels hard to use, the design is the problem, not the user. Foundational to everything I build.
B.J. Fogg
(Stanford Behavior Design Lab)
Behavior = Motivation + Ability + Prompt. This model explains why features fail. It's not enough to build something good; you must align all three elements.
Nielsen Norman Group
(Contextual Inquiry)
Observing real users in their environments beats any survey or focus group. Deployed across every research engagement I've led.
Brad Frost
(Atomic Design)
Component-based thinking for design systems. Enabled scaling consistency across enterprise platforms serving 2,000+ concurrent users.
Tristan Harris
(Center for Humane Technology)
The ethical foundation of design. Behavioral design is powerful; it can manipulate or respect. The choice is intentional. Always chose respect.
Kristin Kohler
(Explainable AI Research)
How to make AI reasoning transparent. Users don't need ML education; they need calibrated trust. Transparency is UI.
Airbnb Design Leadership
(Mobile-first & Systems)
Mobile-first discipline, design systems at scale, and balancing speed with quality. Learned that constraints unlock creativity.
National Security Design Partners
(Intelligence & Law Enforcement)
Working with experts in intelligence analysis, mission planning, and law enforcement. These users can't be fooled. Design must respect their judgment.
